# Operating principles

Chaotic Minds Collective aims to keep creative freedom and practical accountability in balance. These principles describe how work is planned and controlled across the organization.

## Ownership and decision-making

The Owner and Operator retains final decision-making authority for the organization. Work can be researched, analyzed, drafted, and recommended within an agreed scope. Recommendations do not become approvals automatically.

## Approval before consequential changes

Explicit approval is required before consequential actions such as:

- Spending money or starting paid subscriptions.
- Purchasing domains, software, services, or advertising.
- Publishing, launching, or deploying production changes.
- Creating, deleting, transferring, or materially changing repositories or accounts.
- Changing DNS, permissions, payment settings, databases, or production infrastructure.
- Making irreversible, public-facing, or security-sensitive changes.
- Expanding an approved project's scope.

Silence, enthusiasm, or a request to plan does not constitute authorization.

## Project status and evidence

Project records should distinguish between proposed, approved, implemented, tested, and deployed work. A task should not be marked complete merely because a plan, prompt, or draft exists.

Use clear project statuses, record decisions and approvals, and identify dependencies and blockers. Keep paused concepts visible when they may be revisited rather than treating them as abandoned.

## Responsible resource use

Work should account for initial and recurring costs, dependencies, security, privacy, accessibility, and maintainability. Spending and commitments should be considered before they are made, not after.

## Documentation and accountability

Important decisions, changes, and handoffs should be recorded. Documentation should make it possible to understand what changed, why it changed, who approved it, and what remains to be done.

These are organizational operating principles. They do not create employment terms, contractual commitments, or a promise that every project uses an identical workflow.
